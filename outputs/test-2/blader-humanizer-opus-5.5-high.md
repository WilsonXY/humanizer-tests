# Guidelines for AI-assisted writing

## How AI detectors work

AI detectors try to estimate whether a piece of writing resembles text generated by AI. They look at how predictable the word choices are, at patterns in sentence length, structure, vocabulary, and repetition, and at grammar, phrasing, and consistency. They also produce classifier scores, which come from patterns learned by comparing examples of human and AI writing. No detector is perfectly reliable, though. Human writing can be flagged as AI, and AI writing can go undetected.

## Principles for writing with AI

Start with your own ideas. The arguments, opinions, notes, and examples should come from you, and your voice should stay intact, so keep the wording and tone that fit how you naturally communicate. Put substance first: an argument should be specific, relevant, and logical, and it should be supported by evidence. Generic writing is the thing to cut, which usually means repetitive explanations, empty transitions, and vague claims.

Check everything before you rely on it, including facts, citations, and any assumptions the AI made along the way. Keep your notes, drafts, and revision history, since they are evidence of how you actually wrote the piece. And if you are working under academic or professional rules about AI assistance, follow them.

## A workflow that keeps the ideas yours

Write your rough ideas down yourself first. Then ask the AI to organize, clarify, and improve them. Go through each change it makes and correct anything that misrepresents what you think. After that, edit the text for clarity, accuracy, and the voice you want. The last step is to verify the final version and hold on to your drafts.

## A prompt you can reuse

Something like the following works as a standing request:

> Help me improve my writing while preserving my original ideas, reasoning, and intended voice. Improve clarity, structure, grammar, and readability, but do not invent facts, examples, experiences, or references. Avoid generic wording, unnecessary repetition, and excessive formality. Preserve nuance and do not change my intended meaning. Flag unsupported claims, and ask me questions when information is missing. Explain significant changes when that is useful. Prioritize accurate, natural, substantive writing over stylistic polish, and do not guarantee or optimize for any AI detector score.

Use AI to help you express your thinking, and keep the thinking itself your own. Deliberately adding mistakes or swapping words at random to change a detection score is unreliable, and it can make the writing worse.
